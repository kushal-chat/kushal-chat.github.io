Heading east on Interstate 580 near Livermore, California, there is a sign reading **Livermore 17 mi / 27 km** and **Stockton 56 mi / 90 km**. Distances in miles and kilometers, side by side, on an American highway. It is one of very few like it.

## Why it exists

- **The metric push of the early 1990s.** Caltrans installed the sign between 1993 and 1995, after a 1991 Federal Highway Administration push to move highway planning and construction toward metric units.
- **The mandate that disappeared.** A 1995 federal law ended the requirement that states adopt metric standards, and Caltrans went back to English units for project specifications.
- **The survivor.** The sign was still in good shape, so nobody took it down. It outlived the policy that produced it.

Some people, including commenters in a [Bay Area Reddit thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/bayarea/comments/1kyuuu7/anyone_know_why_this_sign_has_kilometers_on_it/), also point out that the sign sits near Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, where scientific work is done in metric. That is a fun coincidence, but the metrication program is the explanation the written sources give.

## Why I like it

Microhistory is the part of history that fits on one object. A sign is cheap, boring and everywhere, and this one is a fossil of a policy argument: the United States started converting, stopped, and left a few kilometers behind in the paint.
